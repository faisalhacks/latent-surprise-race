\section{Introduction}

A world model deployed online has to notice when the world stops behaving as it
expects. The usual signal is the model's own prediction error, and for video
world models this is not folklore: \citet{garrido2025intuitive} show that V-JEPA
detects violations of intuitive physics precisely by comparing the future
against its own latent prediction of it, with ablations pointing to that
mechanism rather than to data or scale.

Separately, \citet{interno2026geophys} show that physical plausibility is
implicitly encoded in the \emph{geometry} of frozen encoder embeddings. So two
signals are available from the same frozen model, and both are known to carry
physical information. The open question is temporal: when a disturbance arrives,
which one crosses an alarm threshold first? If the geometry moves sooner, a
monitor reading it buys reaction time at no extra model cost, which is the
currency that matters for a controller that must stop or re-plan.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
We say \textbf{detection latency} throughout, and avoid the phrase ``early
warning''. Our
benchmark injects each anomaly abruptly at a known frame, so what is measured is
how quickly a monitor reacts to an event that has already occurred --- not
whether it anticipates one. The distinction matters because anticipation is a
different and stronger claim, which this design cannot support.

\paragraph{The comparison is easy to decide in advance by accident.}
Four failure modes sit between a plausible experiment and a trustworthy one, and
each quietly favours a predetermined answer.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The baseline can cheat.} V-JEPA 2's reference implementation runs
one encoder over the whole clip and then uses token masks to split context from
target, so the context representations have already attended to the frames being
predicted. The resulting loss is partly self-fulfilling, which makes the
baseline look \emph{stronger} than it is --- biasing against the hypothesis
under test. We take context from a separate encoder pass whose final tubelet is
overwritten, and verify by assertion that changing the predicted frames leaves
the prediction bit-identical (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\item \textbf{Thresholds can leak.} An alarm threshold fitted on the same clips
used to measure false alarms reports an optimistic operating point. Control
clips are split into disjoint calibration and evaluation sets, and the learned
baseline is cross-fitted within calibration so even its own threshold is honest.

\item \textbf{The registered metric can be degenerate.} Detection latency is
right-censored: clips that never alarm have no observed value. Once fewer than
half the clips alarm, the median is pinned at the censoring value \emph{for
every monitor}, and the comparison returns ``no difference'' regardless of the
truth. We kept the median as the registered primary because it was specified in
advance, and registered a censoring-robust secondary alongside it before running
anything, so a degenerate primary is visible rather than silently null.

\item \textbf{The verdict can be vacuous.} If the prediction-error baseline does
not detect the anomalies at all --- if its detection rate equals its own
false-alarm rate --- then ``geometry is faster'' is true in the way that
anything is faster than never. This is not hypothetical:
\citet{pavuluri2026asleep} report exactly this collapse for a V-JEPA-based
novelty signal, which looked effective across datasets but fell to chance within
one. We therefore report a \emph{lift} for every monitor, flag any baseline
firing at chance, and mark an otherwise-passing verdict \textsc{vacuous}. To our
knowledge this check is not standard practice in representation-based anomaly
detection, though the underlying phenomenon is documented.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we find.}
The v1 experiment returns a \textbf{negative} verdict --- classified as
\textbf{exploratory} per reviewer ruling (2026-10-12), not confirmatory, for
the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:results} --- and the shape of
the negative is the contribution. Probes A and B alarm significantly sooner than
$\mathcal{L}_0$, the model's own pretraining objective --- up to $1.88$ frames
of \RMTA{} at Holm-adjusted $p = 0.003$, replicating across both operating
points and corroborated by the log-rank test. On that evidence alone the
hypothesis looks confirmed.

It is not. $\mathcal{L}_1$, a ridge map from $[z_{t-1}; z_{t-2}]$ to $z_t$
fitted on control clips, reads exactly the latent sequence the probes read and
beats every one of them by $2.05$ to $6.60$ frames. The anomaly signal is
present in the latents; the three geometric statistics are simply worse
readouts of it than a two-frame linear extrapolation. Neither baseline fires at
chance and the primary metric is not degenerate, so this null is informative
rather than an artefact of a weak comparison.

\paragraph{What this contributes.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A pre-registered protocol for detection latency in frozen video world
models, with causality enforced structurally --- monitors are streaming objects
that cannot reference the future --- and verified adversarially by inverting
every frame after $t$ and asserting every score is unchanged.
\item A benchmark of four anomaly families injected at a fixed frame into a
controlled physical scene, with matched pre-injection histories so comparisons
are paired at the clip level. We characterise what each family actually
measures, including an occlusion condition that turns out to probe object
permanence rather than partial corruption (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).
\item An evaluation that treats detection latency as survival analysis ---
Kaplan--Meier curves, log-rank, paired Wilcoxon, and a restricted mean with a
paired cluster bootstrap --- under two threshold regimes, with multiplicity
control across all comparisons.
\item A model-agnostic adapter contract, demonstrated on two architectures from
opposite sides of the pixel/latent divide, with a parameterised test suite that
every registered adapter must pass. Implementing the second one showed that the
leakage trap is architecture-specific: VideoMAE leaks by a completely different
route (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}).
\item Five documented failure modes, each made structurally impossible, tested
adversarially, or surfaced as a reported diagnostic.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{On reporting a null result.}
The protocol commits in advance to a decision gate and to writing up a null
result if no probe clears it. We regard that commitment as the main reason to
trust whatever the gate eventually returns, and the harness is built so that a
null answer is as publishable, and as informative, as a positive one.
