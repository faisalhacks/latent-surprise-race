\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Predictive world models.}
Learned models that roll the world forward in a latent space underpin
model-based control \citep{ha2018world,hafner2020dream}, and the
joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) family makes the prediction
explicitly representational rather than pixel-level
\citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}. V-JEPA extends this to video
\citep{bardes2024vjepa} and V-JEPA 2 scales it \citep{assran2025vjepa2}. We
treat V-JEPA 2 as frozen throughout: the question is what its representations
already encode, not what they could be trained to encode.

\paragraph{Prediction error as the established physics signal.}
\citet{garrido2025intuitive} show that V-JEPA acquires intuitive physics from
self-supervised pretraining alone, and crucially that it detects physical
violations \emph{by comparing the future against its own prediction of it} ---
latent prediction error is the mechanism, and ablations point to it rather than
to data or scale. That result is the reason our $\mathcal{L}_0$ baseline is the
model's own pretraining objective rather than a proxy: the prediction-error
signal is not a straw man, it is the published mechanism, and a protocol that
weakened it would be testing nothing.

\paragraph{Geometry of frozen embeddings already carries physical information.}
\citet{interno2026geophys} show that physical plausibility is implicitly
captured by geometric properties of per-frame embeddings from frozen image
encoders, with strong violation-detection results and correspondence to human
responses to object-permanence violations. This matters for how our
contribution should be read. That representation geometry encodes physical
plausibility is established; we do not claim it. The open question we address is
\emph{temporal}: given that both the geometry and the prediction error carry
the signal, which one crosses an alarm threshold sooner, measured under a
protocol where neither side has been quietly advantaged. Relatedly,
\citet{kriegeskorte2008rsa} established comparing representations by their
geometry rather than their values, which motivates our probe~B.

\paragraph{Occlusion, and what our occlusion condition actually tests.}
\citet{xie2026tracking} probe exactly the regime our occlusion family lands in:
a frozen V-JEPA 2 predictor forgets moving hidden objects within about
$0.3$~seconds, while the encoder retains hidden-object information for far
longer. Our occlusion condition hides the disc completely in \lgwOccFullyHidden{}
of post-injection frames (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}), so it is an object-permanence
probe, not a partial-corruption probe --- and their encoder/predictor asymmetry
predicts that a monitor reading encoder geometry and a monitor reading the
predictor's error need not behave alike on it. We flag this as a confound to
interpret against rather than a prediction we tested.

\paragraph{Self-supervised novelty signals can collapse to chance.}
\citet{pavuluri2026asleep} evaluate a V-JEPA-based scheme that flags clips with
unpredictable embeddings as interesting. It appeared to work across datasets,
but the effect was domain shift: within a single dataset performance fell to
chance, while a lightly supervised probe on the same frozen features roughly
doubled average precision. Their diagnosis --- the representation was adequate,
the self-supervised objective was the limitation --- is the direct precedent for
the guard we describe in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}. A monitor whose detection
rate equals its own false-alarm rate is firing at chance, and a probe that
``beats'' such a baseline has demonstrated nothing. To our knowledge this check
is not standard in representation-based anomaly detection; our contribution is
to make it a reported quantity and to mark an otherwise-passing verdict vacuous
when it fails, not to have noticed the phenomenon first.

\paragraph{Monitoring latents for failures, and calibrating the threshold.}
\citet{zhang2026foresight} detect failures in long-horizon manipulation from
action-conditioned world-model latents using only episode-level labels, and
calibrate detection thresholds with conformal prediction. \citet{samad2026puls}
work in the complementary direction, showing that \emph{anticipated} V-JEPA 2
latents are more semantically separable than observed ones and reporting an
anticipatory advantage at half a second. Both monitor world-model latents, as we
do; both differ from this work in that neither isolates the latent signal
against the model's own prediction error under a frozen protocol with matched
false-alarm rates, which is the specific comparison we are trying to make
trustworthy.

\paragraph{Compression of world models.}
\citet{fu2026quantization} study post-training quantization of DINO-WM and
report asymmetric sensitivity across components, plus failure modes that further
optimization does not repair. That asymmetry is the motivation for the
compression study we have drafted but deliberately not run
(Section~\ref{sec:future}): if encoder and predictor degrade differently, a
geometry monitor and a prediction-error monitor should degrade differently too,
and a per-anomaly latency measurement would show it where an aggregate score
would not.

\paragraph{Detection latency as a survival quantity.}
How quickly a monitor fires is a time-to-event quantity, and some episodes are
never detected, so their latency is right-censored and a plain mean or median
over observed alarms is biased. We use Kaplan--Meier estimation
\citep{kaplan1958nonparametric} and the log-rank test
\citep{mantel1966evaluation}, and --- because a median is degenerate under heavy
censoring --- a restricted mean detection latency, the analogue of restricted
mean survival time \citep{royston2013restricted}. Paired comparisons use the
Wilcoxon signed-rank test \citep{wilcoxon1945individual} and a cluster bootstrap
\citep{efron1979bootstrap}, with Holm's correction \citep{holm1979simple}.
Survival analysis is uncommon in anomaly-detector evaluation, which more often
reports AUROC at a fixed horizon; we argue it is the right frame whenever the
quantity of interest is \emph{how quickly}, not merely \emph{whether}.

\paragraph{Pre-registration in machine learning.}
Freezing hypotheses, metrics and decision rules before observing data is routine
in clinical trials and rare here, where the same data typically serves
exploration and confirmation. We adopt it because the hypothesis is attractive,
the design space is large, and the number of defensible-looking analysis choices
is more than sufficient to manufacture a positive result by accident. Every
departure from the frozen protocol is logged with its date and provenance
(Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}).
