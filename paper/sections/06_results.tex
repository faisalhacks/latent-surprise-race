\subsection{The registered verdict}

\textbf{\lgwVerdict.} No geometry probe satisfies the frozen gate criterion.
Two diagnostics say this null is informative rather than an artefact:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Neither baseline fires at chance} (\lgwBaselinesAtChance{} flagged).
$\mathcal{L}_0$ detects $36.6\%$ of anomalous clips against a $14.0\%$ held-out
clip-level false-alarm rate (lift $+0.226$); $\mathcal{L}_1$ detects $70.8\%$
against $10.0\%$ (lift $+0.608$). So the probes were compared against monitors
that genuinely work, and the verdict is not vacuous.
\item \textbf{The primary metric is not degenerate} (\lgwMedianDegenerate).
$\mathcal{L}_1$ has a median detection latency of $4$ frames, well inside the
window, so the median could have separated the monitors. It did not, because
there was nothing to separate in the probes' favour.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What actually happened: the probes beat the wrong baseline}

The headline number is not that the probes lost, but \emph{how}
(Tables~\ref{tab:tta} and~\ref{tab:cmp}).

\paragraph{Probes A and B are genuinely faster than the model's own loss.}
Against $\mathcal{L}_0$, probe~B gains $+1.88$ frames of \RMTA{}
(95\% CI $[+1.48, +2.26]$, Holm-adjusted $p = 0.003$) and probe~A gains $+0.98$
frames ($[+0.70, +1.26]$, $p = 0.003$). Both are significant, both replicate at
the clip-matched operating point, and the log-rank test agrees. Read in
isolation, this is exactly the positive result the hypothesis predicted.

\paragraph{But a ridge regression on the same latents beats all three probes
decisively.} $\mathcal{L}_1$ --- a linear map from $[z_{t-1}; z_{t-2}]$ to
$z_t$, fitted on control clips only --- reaches a median detection latency of
$4$ frames where every probe remains censored at $14$, and an \RMTA{} of $6.45$
against the best probe's $8.49$. The probes lose to it by $2.05$ to $6.60$
frames of \RMTA{}, at $p$-values below $10^{-39}$ two-sided.

Since the gate requires beating \emph{both} baselines, the verdict is negative.
We think that is the right call, and the reason is the point of the result:
\textbf{$\mathcal{L}_1$ consumes exactly the sequence $\{z_t\}$ that the probes
consume.} It sees no extra information. It is a two-frame linear extrapolation,
about as simple a readout as the latent sequence admits. That it dominates three
hand-designed geometric statistics says the anomaly signal is present in the
latents and that curvature, representational distance and covariance shift are
simply \emph{worse ways of reading it out} --- not that geometry carries
something prediction error misses.

\paragraph{Had we omitted $\mathcal{L}_1$, this paper would have reported a
positive result.} Probes A and B beat the model's own pretraining objective at
$p = 0.003$ with confidence intervals excluding zero, under two operating
points and two tests. The conclusion ``latent geometry signals anomalies before
prediction error does'' would have been defensible, reviewable, and wrong in the
way that matters: the effect is attributable to $\mathcal{L}_0$ being an
insensitive monitor on this benchmark, not to geometry being special. The
information-matched baseline was registered precisely to make that
distinction, and it is the single component that changed the conclusion.

\subsection{Where detection happens at all}

The per-condition breakdown (Table~\ref{tab:cond}) shows the pooled numbers
average over very different regimes.

\emph{Frame drop} is detected quickly by everything ($\mathcal{L}_1$ \RMTA{}
$0.98$, probe~A $1.45$, $\mathcal{L}_0$ $2.23$): a held frame makes the next
latent nearly identical to the last, which both a predictor and a curvature
statistic register at once.

\emph{Occlusion} separates the monitors most sharply: $\mathcal{L}_1$ reaches
$1.50$ while $\mathcal{L}_0$ is at $13.59$, barely detecting it. Recall that
this condition hides the disc completely in \lgwOccFullyHidden{} of
post-injection frames, so it is an object-permanence probe
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}). \citet{xie2026tracking} report that V-JEPA 2's
predictor forgets moving hidden objects within about $0.3$~seconds while its
encoder retains them far longer --- which is consistent with a monitor reading
pooled encoder states ($\mathcal{L}_1$, and the probes) outperforming one
reading the predictor's error ($\mathcal{L}_0$) on exactly this family. We
flag the consistency; we did not test the mechanism.

\emph{Gravity inversion} and \emph{friction} are barely detected by anything
(all \RMTA{} between $12.6$ and $13.7$). A $15$-frame window is simply too short
for a change in acceleration to move the representation much, which is the
limitation we anticipated for these families rather than a discovery.

So the pooled result is driven by frame drop and occlusion; the two genuinely
physical perturbations are close to undetectable at this horizon for every
monitor we tested, including the model's own loss.

\subsection{Numerical precision is not neutral between the two monitor families}
\label{sec:precision}

Before the registered run we compared fp32, bf16 and fp16 on the real weights
(Table~\ref{tab:precision}). Reduced precision does not degrade the monitors
evenly. Under bf16 the worst geometry probe shifts by a median
\lgwPrecWorstProbeBf{} while $\mathcal{L}_0$ shifts by \lgwPrecWorstLossBf{} ---
about a factor of \lgwPrecRatioBf{}. The mechanism is structural:
$\mathcal{L}_0$ is a mean over $H'W' \times d$ values, so independent rounding
error averages away, whereas probe~A is a second difference of nearby vectors
and probe~B a cosine against a mean of five nearly identical vectors. Both
amplify exactly the error the loss suppresses.

Choosing a reduced precision for throughput would therefore have handicapped the
probes while leaving the baseline essentially intact --- a \emph{directional}
bias against the hypothesis, introduced by what looks like an implementation
detail and invisible to any aggregate accuracy check. We ran the registered
experiment in fp32 as pre-registered, at roughly $2.9\times$ the cost per clip
($7554$ GPU-seconds for $1200$ clips on an A10G).

We flag this as a general hazard: whenever a pooled output error is compared
against a higher-order statistic of internal states, the two sides have
different numerical conditioning, and precision is part of the experimental
design rather than a free parameter.

\subsection{Harness verification}

Independently of the outcome: every monitor is a streaming object that cannot
reference a future frame, verified by inverting all frames after a cut point and
asserting bit-identity for earlier ones, against the real architecture; the
$\mathcal{L}_0$ prediction does not move when only the predicted frames change
but does move when $f_{t-2}$ changes; probe values match their closed forms to
$10^{-9}$ and the $O(k^2d)$ covariance identity matches the naive computation to
machine precision; thresholds are a function of calibration controls alone; and
both registered adapters pass the identical contract suite.

\subsection{The exploratory CPU pilot was superseded}
\label{sec:pilot}

Before GPU access was available we began a reduced-resolution pilot on CPU
($128$~px, \lgwPilotTarget{} clips). It reached \lgwPilotTotal{} clips
(\lgwPilotCalib{} calibration controls, \lgwPilotEval{} evaluation controls,
\lgwPilotAnom{} anomalous) and was stopped when the registered run became
possible. **No outcome was ever computed from it**: thresholds are calibrated on
control clips, and its control splits never completed, so no threshold existed
and no detection latency was defined. It is reported here only so that its
existence is on the record.
