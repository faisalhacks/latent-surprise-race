\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{One architecture, one synthetic domain.}
The registered experiment covers frozen V-JEPA 2 on bouncing discs. Whether any
effect is a property of joint-embedding predictive architectures, of
transformers over video generally, or of this particular scene is not decided
by this experiment, and we do not claim otherwise. A VideoMAE adapter exists and
passes the contract, but \emph{has not been run}: an adapter is a capability,
not a result, and we are careful not to present one as the other. A
physics-simulator domain is the other obvious next step.

\paragraph{Mean pooling discards where the anomaly is.}
Both the latent state and the $\mathcal{L}_0$ loss average over the spatial
token grid --- $1024$ dimensions for the latent, $H'W'd$ values for the loss.
Our anomalies are spatially local, so averaging dilutes them. This is a
sensitivity cost borne by both monitor families, but not necessarily
symmetrically, and it is the most likely explanation if $\mathcal{L}_0$ turns
out to have little dynamic range. Max-pooling, or restricting the loss to tokens
whose content actually changed, are natural variants we did not register.

\paragraph{Pooled thresholds assume stationarity in $t$.}
For $t<15$ the causal window is left-padded by repeating $f_0$, so warm-up
latents are computed on partly synthetic windows. The score distribution is
therefore mildly $t$-dependent, which widens the pooled calibration
distribution and costs sensitivity. Controls and anomalies pass through an
identical pipeline, so this cannot manufacture a difference \emph{between}
monitors. A per-frame threshold would be the cleaner estimator.

\paragraph{Probes A and C are not scale-invariant, and may carry per-clip
offsets.} In a preliminary two-clip check on real weights, probe~C differed by
roughly $8\%$ between clips while moving only a few percent \emph{within} each.
With a threshold pooled across clips, a monitor whose between-clip variance
exceeds its within-clip signal behaves partly as a per-clip constant rather than
as a change detector. Probe~B, scale-invariant by construction, showed no
comparable offset. Standardising each probe against its own pre-injection frames
is the standard remedy; it cannot be applied uniformly here because probe~C is
undefined before frame $15$ and so has no within-clip reference window. We flag
this rather than patch it, because changing a frozen probe definition after
seeing data is exactly what pre-registration exists to prevent.

\paragraph{$\dt$ is quantised by the tubelet.}
Since $z_t$ summarises frames $(t{-}1,t)$, latencies should not be read as
single-frame reaction times. Both monitor families inherit the same
quantisation, so the comparison is unaffected, but the absolute numbers are
coarser than the units suggest.

\paragraph{The log-rank test is mis-specified here.}
Both arms come from the same clips. We report it because it is the standard
censoring-aware two-sample test and its agreement or disagreement with the
paired analyses is informative, but the paired Wilcoxon and the paired cluster
bootstrap are the tests that respect the design.

\paragraph{A negative result would be about these probes, not about geometry.}
If no probe clears the gate, the honest conclusion is that \emph{these three
second-order summaries of a mean-pooled latent} do not warn earlier than
prediction error on \emph{this} benchmark --- not that no geometric signal
exists. The space of candidate probes is large and we tested three fixed ones,
chosen in advance precisely so that we could not search it.
